Sheet 01 reworked the editor's panels. This sheet steps out one level. The agent doesn't decorate the editor — it drives the whole app: it navigates between decks, swaps slides, applies themes, reads your selection (see navigation, app-theme-command, pending-selection-context bridges in AppShell.tsx). So it can't be a panel inside one view. It's a peer of the app — a persistent column that stays put while the view underneath it changes.

Design outside-in: get the shell frame right first — left nav, agent peer, top bar — then the editor is just one view that slots into the middle. Borrow Agent Native's own slide-template shell: structured nav, an Extensions tray you can extend ad-hoc, a workspace switcher, and a Chat peer that's always reachable.

Nav is two icons

The rail holds only Decks and Theme as bare icons plus a collapse button. No Team, no Design Systems, no sense of the product's surface area.

2

Agent is opt-in, not a peer

It's a conditional <aside> behind a toggle button buried in the rail. The thing that can drive the whole app reads as an optional add-on.

3

No extensions, no workspace

No way to add a capability ad-hoc, no workspace/account anchor at the foot of the rail. The shell can't grow with the user.

The architecture already supports a peer — the agent is a sibling of <main>. It just isn't presented as one.

Every bar collapses

Each region folds independently and remembers its state. On a laptop, collapse what you don't need — the canvas always takes the reclaimed width. Nothing unmounts; it just narrows.
